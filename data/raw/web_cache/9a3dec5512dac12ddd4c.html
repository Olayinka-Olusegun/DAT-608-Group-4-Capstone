]]> Zamfara Moves to Halt Illegal Drug Sales, Hawking https://dailytrust.com/zamfara-moves-to-halt-illegal-drug-sales-hawking/ Sat, 15 Aug 2026 16:42:19 +0000 https://dailytrust.com/?p=1940846 Abdullahi Chakwa

The Zamfara State Government has intensified efforts to curb the sale and hawking of medicines illegally, describing the practice as a major threat to public health and efforts to improve healthcare delivery in the state.

The state Commissioner for Health, Dr Nafisa Muhammad Maradun, disclosed this at a stakeholders’ meeting in Gusau on the regulation and enforcement of laws governing the sale and distribution of medicines.

Maradun, who was represented by the Permanent Secretary of the ministry, Alhaji Rilwanu Musa Zurmi, said the government’s investments in healthcare infrastructure and manpower could be undermined if residents continued to patronise fake, substandard or improperly handled medicines.

She said, “Our infrastructure has been developed, but the major challenge is that after all these investments in the health sector, a client may end up with fake medicine, and that is a great challenge in our state.”

  • Troops Kill 2 Terrorists, Intercept 180 Explosives in Zamfara

The commissioner warned that indiscriminate drug hawking could expose residents to wrong dosages, inappropriate use of antibiotics, antimicrobial resistance and treatment failure.

According to her, the government’s strategy would combine tighter regulation of the pharmaceutical supply chain, public enlightenment and stronger enforcement against individuals and groups involved in illegal medicine sales.

The Director of Pharmaceutical Services in the ministry, Pharmacist Mas’ud Isah, explained that existing laws and regulatory provisions prohibit the hawking and unauthorised distribution of medicines.

He specifically highlighted the mandates of the National Agency for Food and Drug Administration and Control (NAFDAC) and other relevant regulatory bodies, urging stakeholders to ensure effective implementation of the laws.

Isah also called for increased intelligence sharing among regulatory agencies, security forces, traditional institutions and market associations to identify and apprehend those involved in illegal medicine distribution.

He said the objective was not to target legitimate pharmaceutical businesses but to protect residents from the dangers associated with fake, adulterated, expired and improperly stored medicines.

The meeting was attended by representatives of the Pharmacy Council of Nigeria (PCN), NAFDAC, the National Association of Patent and Proprietary Medicine Dealers (NAPMED), the Association of Community Pharmacists of Nigeria (ACPN), the police, Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC), religious and traditional institutions, as well as labour unions.

The stakeholders pledged their support for the state government’s initiative, stressing the need for coordinated action to protect residents and strengthen compliance with regulations governing the handling and sale of medicines.

“Essentially, that’s what it is. We have the blockade. No ships get through unless we want them to”, he added.

Iran swiftly rejected the claim, with Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi insisting that the strait “has been Iranian, is Iranian, and will remain Iranian.”

However, a White House official later described Trump’s territorial declaration as a joke.

He said the president had not discussed the idea with his advisers.

The remarks came as efforts to reopen the vital shipping route remained deadlocked.

Tanker traffic through the strait has reduced to a fraction of its normal level.

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said on Saturday that Tehran had not decided to resume negotiations with Washington.

The Strait of Hormuz, which separates Iran from Oman and links the Persian Gulf with the Gulf of Oman, is one of the world’s most important energy chokepoints.

The International Energy Agency said nearly 15 million barrels of crude oil a day passed through the waterway in 2025, representing about 34 per cent of global crude oil trade.

The waterway has been at the centre of the US Iran confrontation since the war began in February, with shipping traffic falling sharply as Iran asserted control over the passage and the US maintained a naval blockade.

Reuters reported that only nine commodity vessels transited the strait on Thursday, compared with an August daily average of 12.

Iran has continued to insist that the strait remains under its control, while Washington has demanded the restoration of unrestricted shipping.
