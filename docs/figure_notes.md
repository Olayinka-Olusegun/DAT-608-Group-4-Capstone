Figure 1, coverage curve. The vertical axis is the share of LGAs that were actually attacked in a week which appear in the top k of that week's ranking. Covering 20 of 774 areas, that is 2.6% of the country, reaches 36% of the week's attacks, and covering 50 areas reaches 61%. The same 20 area budget allocated by last week's incident report, which is the current practice the brief describes, reaches 11%. The gap between those two lines is the operational value of the model, and it is widest exactly where a state government has to operate, at small k.

Figure 2, calibration. Each point is one decile of the score distribution, plotted as predicted rate against the rate actually observed. Points on the diagonal mean the number can be read as a probability. The largest deviation across the ten deciles is 0.001, and the top decile predicts 0.036 against an observed 0.037. This is what allows the tiers to carry a stable meaning: severe is not merely the top of the list, it corresponds to a rate that holds up out of sample.

Figure 3, weekly capture. The upper line counts LGAs attacked each week and the lower line counts how many of those the top twenty had already flagged. The two move together, which is what a usable early warning looks like, but the list caught nothing at all in 27 of 105 weeks. Those weeks are the honest limit of the approach: they are dominated by attacks in LGAs with no recent history, where a self-exciting model has nothing to excite from and only the wider feeds could help.

Figure 4, drivers. Bars are the mean absolute SHAP contribution across all 774 areas in the scoring week. The single largest is baseline rate for this lga, and features derived from the point process account for 78% of the total attribution. That is the clearest statement of what the hybrid is doing: the tree is mostly arbitrating between the components of the Hawkes intensity and adjusting them with calendar and operational context, rather than rediscovering temporal clustering from lagged counts.

Figure 5, the fitted point process. The left panel shows how quickly the effect of an attack decays, with a half life of 22 days, so the elevated period after an incident runs to roughly six weeks rather than the one week a monthly incident report implies. The right panel decomposes the branching ratio of 0.50: about 50% of recorded events are triggered by an earlier event, and of that triggered share 82% stays inside the same LGA while the rest crosses into a neighbour. That cross-border fraction is the quantitative form of the displacement the brief describes, and it is why the neighbour terms carry real weight in the model.
